1. The idea

A carry trade buys high-yielding assets and funds them with low-yielding ones. It earns a risk premium on average, but the premium is compensation for crash risk: when many investors hold the same trade and are forced to unwind at once, losses are sudden and large (Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen 2008).

We test this mechanism in two markets with the same economic structure but very different investors:

2. Strategy A: crowded G10 carry

Rule. At each month-end, rank JPY, EUR, GBP, CHF, CAD, AUD and NZD by their 3-month rate minus the US rate. Go long the 2 highest and short the 2 lowest, equal weights, dollar-neutral. Returns include the interest differential (covered interest parity). Costs: 3 bp per unit traded.

Crowding filter. CFTC Commitments of Traders: net speculative position / open interest, z-scored over 36 months and used only after its Friday release. Portfolio crowding = average z of the long leg minus that of the short leg. When crowding > 1, positions are cut to half. In-sample ends 2010; 2011 onward is out-of-sample.

Does crowding predict bad carry months?

Next-month carry averages 0.30% after uncrowded months but only 0.02% after crowded months, with more negative skew. The filter lifts the out-of-sample Sharpe ratio (0.27 → 0.32 net) but does not avoid the 2008 crash, which began from a non-crowded reading.

3. Strategy C: crypto funding carry

Rule. Universe: 12 Binance USDT perpetuals, including LUNA and FTT, which collapsed, to avoid survivorship bias (USDC is used only as a sanity check). A coin is tradable only on days with a price and positive volume. Every Sunday, rank coins by their trailing 7-day average funding rate; short the top third, long the bottom third, equal weights, dollar-neutral. Shorts receive positive funding. Costs: 5 bp per unit traded. In-sample 2020–2023, out-of-sample 2024 onward.

Risk: who causes the losses?

Beta to BTC is essentially zero (−0.02, t = −0.75): the strategy is market-neutral. Its risk comes from individual coins. Profit and loss by coin, in percentage points:

Worst weeks and a pre-specified risk control

Fixed before running it: cap each coin at 1/6 of its leg, and do not hold as a long any coin whose 7-day funding is below −50% a year (a “distress” filter).

Honest result: the control halves losses in the LUNA and FTX weeks, but mainly because the cap leaves about half the capital unused, so the Sharpe ratio falls. LUNA's funding crossed −50% only mid-week (10 May 2022), after the weekly rebalance, and the filter also blocked the two biggest winners (BNB, SOL). In crypto, deeply negative funding usually signals crowded shorts, whose forced covering pushes prices up, not distress. Crypto carry's crash risk is the mirror image of FX: it ends up long whatever traders short hardest.

4. Combined portfolio

Each strategy is scaled to 10% volatility using only past volatility (36 months for A, 12 for C), then combined 50/50.
